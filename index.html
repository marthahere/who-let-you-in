HOW THIS WORKS

Applicants fill out a form. Some of what they say is true and some it isn't. Your job is to figure out which is which before you decide.

  • Records — The official file on this person, including names, birthdays and schools. Sometimes more than one person shows up with a similar name.
  • Digital Footprint — Take a look at some of their posts, comments and replies.
  • Account History — When the account was made and previous usernames.
  • Connected People — References the applicant listed. Call them. Ask about the applicant. Observe if something doesn't add up.

You are provided with a space to take notes as you go. When you've made up your mind: ACCEPT or REJECT.

FIRST SHIFT

You're covering the access desk for the Velora community this week. The previous moderator left without much of a handoff, so there isn't much information available beyond the usual verification process.

Velora is an online community for people in town. Membership is private, and every new account has to be verified before access is granted. The reason for the extra screening isn't explained in the information provided to moderators.

For each application, you'll have the information submitted by the applicant, any available public records, their online activity, and the contacts they've provided. Use those to determine whether the applicant can be verified.

You choose whether to accept or reject the applicant. Just remember: once you let someone in, they're in.
